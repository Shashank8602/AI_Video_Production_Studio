# Video 001 — Olbers' paradox: why is the night sky dark?

Status: research complete, script awaiting user review.

## Claims used in the script

| # | Claim | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In an endless, unchanging (static, uniform) universe full of stars, every line of sight would end on a star, so the sky should be as bright as a stellar surface | Wikipedia (Olbers' paradox); UNLV Jeffery; AMNH |
| 2 | The universe has a finite age, about 13.8 billion years (Planck 2018: 13.797 ± 0.023 Gyr) | UNLV Jeffery (Planck 2018); Wikipedia |
| 3 | Light from the most distant stars has not had time to reach us; this finite age is the main resolution | Sky at Night; Wikipedia; UWA; Scientific American |
| 4 | Expansion stretches the wavelength of light (redshift), making it fainter and shifting it toward infrared | Wikipedia; HyperPhysics; Sky at Night |
| 5 | Looking out is looking back in time (light travel time) | General astronomy; follows from claim 3 |

## Notes and cautions

- **Redshift contribution is contested in popular sources.** One analysis (Tim Andersen, blog) estimates redshift explains about 60% (±10%) of the darkness; Sky at Night says it is not enough on its own. The script therefore says expansion makes light "fainter and redder" without claiming it is the main reason. Finite age is the headline.
- **Star lifetimes also matter** (AMNH): even an infinitely old universe would not have enough fuel to keep stars shining forever. Not used in the script to keep it short.
- **History** (optional extra): Heinrich Olbers wrote about it in 1823 but was not the first; Edgar Allan Poe proposed a finite-age explanation in 1848 (UWA). Not used in the script.
- "A clue that the universe had a beginning": consistent with the finite-age resolution; phrased as a clue, not proof.
- Avoid saying the dark gaps are "the future". The light simply has not arrived yet.
